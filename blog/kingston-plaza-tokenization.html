Kingston Plaza: A Token Is a Claim, Not the Building

A token is not an asset. It’s a claim on an asset. The governance job is making sure that claim can’t come loose.

What happened

This is a simulated scenario I designed for practice.

The owners of a commercial plaza in Kingston, Jamaica, want to split it into 1,000 digital tokens and sell them to institutional investors. The question: how do you link those tokens to a physical building in a way serious investors will trust?

The technician’s view

Write a smart contract, mint 1,000 tokens that represent the building, and build a website where investors can buy them.

That produces tokens. It doesn’t produce anything an investor can rely on. Nothing in the code gives a token holder a legal right to the building.

The architect’s view

The core risk is decoupling: the token keeps trading while the legal right to the asset behind it is missing, disputed or out of date. I looked at it through the three pillars of a digital asset:

  • Core data: each token must point to the building’s official record. In Jamaica that means the National Land Agency title (its volume and folio), plus the property’s mapped boundaries.
  • Metadata: investors are paid from occupancy and net operating income. That data needs to arrive from the property management system on a schedule, so the token doesn’t carry stale or invented numbers.
  • Legal and access layer: the token can’t literally be a piece of the building. A special purpose company holds the title through a trustee, and the tokens are shares in that company.

The control: Institutional-Grade Tokenization Framework

  1. Hard anchor: every token maps to the government land record for the property.
  2. Live synchronization: occupancy and income data flow automatically from the property management software into the token platform.
  3. Legal wrapper: a special purpose company and a trust deed bind the smart contract’s rules to how the physical building is actually managed.

One thing to check early: shares in a company sold to investors are usually treated as securities, so the securities regulator’s rules apply. That’s a question for a lawyer, not a developer.

How to prove it works

An auditor should be able to take any token, trace it to the company’s share register, trace the company to the title held by the trustee, and match the income figures on the platform against the property manager’s books.

Takeaway

Coding a token takes an afternoon. Keeping the claim tied to the asset takes governance.

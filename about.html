About me

I’m Domonick Thompson. I work at the point where digital records meet physical and institutional value, and I’m building toward a career in governance.

Where I come from

My background is hands-on. I hold an Associate and a Bachelor’s degree in Information and Communication Technology, majoring in Network Engineering and Security, and I completed the Cisco CCNA and CCNP coursework.

After a six-month internship, I spent two and a half years on contract at an IT solutions firm. I:

  • found the firm’s first office and ran the build-out from bare shell to working space
  • managed inventory and the vehicle fleet
  • deployed fixed and mobile networks
  • digitized documentation workflows
  • handled project management, client relations and sales

Since then I’ve been building my security skills in my own lab: purple-team exercises, detection engineering, and self-hosted monitoring.

What I’m working toward

Most people think managing digital assets means organizing files. I think it means keeping a digital claim in sync with the real value behind it. The record has to match reality, and someone has to be able to prove it does.

I approach that in three parts:

  • Identify and value what an organization owns and what depends on it (asset management, ISO 55001)
  • Protect those assets from risk (security and risk management, CISM)
  • Verify that the records tell the truth (an auditor’s lens)

I’m studying for ISO 55001 Lead Auditor and preparing for CISM.

What you’ll find here

The blog is my working record. Each article comes from a challenge: a failure, the quick technical fix, and the governance control that stops it from happening again. Articles are labeled as either simulated scenarios I designed for practice or real-world autopsies based only on public sources.

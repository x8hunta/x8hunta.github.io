Working with AI taught me to verify twice

Banks call it maker-checker: one makes, another checks. Here one AI built it, a second AI checked it, and I checked the checker.

The story

I write up each network lab as a slide deck, and an AI helps build them. So I asked a second AI (Gemma) to compare each deck with the text I had approved. Twice, on two decks, the same thing happened:

  • It got every hard fact right: addresses, ports, ping results, commands.
  • It found wording I never approved. The first AI had quietly added its own headings and labels. On the second deck, about nine lines got through.
  • Most of what it called “missing” wasn’t missing. On the first deck, all 10 items were there. On the second, 5 of 9 were.

So the second AI is useful, but only as a second opinion that a person checks. That’s why my decks now say “wording review pending” until I sign off.

What it proves

A second AI is worth running, and its output still needs checking.

What it doesn’t prove yet

  • Two runs, two decks, one model. That’s a pattern to watch, not a measured result.
  • The first run’s exact prompt wasn’t saved. The second run’s was.
  • Many false “missing” items came from how the text was pulled out of the slides, not only from the AI.
